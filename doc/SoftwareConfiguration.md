## Servo maximum-speed configuration

The team identifies the supply as the [goBILDA 6V Servo Power Injector, 3125-0001-0001](https://www.gobilda.com/6v-servo-power-injector-6-channel-8-15v-input/). Its six channels share a regulated 6 V output rated up to 24 A total from an 8–15 V input; this is not 24 A per channel. The manufacturer specifies full 24 A output for 2 minutes 30 seconds at room temperature, with current/thermal limiting. The injector supplies the Axon servos; the gate now connects directly to Expansion Hub servo port 3 and is excluded from this 6 V supply. Physical load/timing checks remain deferred.

The original five servos remain in positional mode. Gate, depollinator, hood and home use positional targets. The linear slide uses a single **continuous-mode** Axon MAX+ (`slide_servo`, Control Hub servo port 2 since 2026-11-19; formerly Expansion Hub servo port 5) with its analog feedback on Control Hub analog port 0 (`slide_encoder`; formerly Expansion Hub analog port 0). The shot blocker is permanently attached to the slide; the claw (`claw_servo`) was removed on 2026-11-19. The slide servo's Axon programmer mode must be set to continuous; its position comes from counted analog turns, not a servo position target. Turret commands have brief smoothing; competition manual velocity uses two 10-ms filters and the position path uses 5-ms smoothing. Full-stick manual yaw uses the MAX+ rated shaft speed through the 1:1 turret gearing; smaller stick inputs intentionally request proportionally slower movement. Hood button steps select a destination; they do not impose a servo speed limit. Mechanical angle limits remain enforced.

| Mechanism | Servo | Rated shaft speed at 6.0 V | Mechanism travel estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turret rotation, ports 1 and 2 | Axon MAX+ | 0.115 s/60 degrees (521.74 deg/s) | 521.74 deg/s at 1:1 |
| Hood, port 0 | Axon MAX+ | 0.115 s/60 degrees | 260.87 deg/s through 2:1 linkage; 0.0575 s for 15 degrees of hood movement |
| Depollinator, port 4 | Axon MAX+ | 0.115 s/60 degrees | 0.25875 s for 135 degrees |
| Gate, port 3 (direct Hub; 6 V catalog reference only) | goBILDA 2000-0025-0004 Super Speed | 0.043 s/60 degrees (1395.35 deg/s) | 0.0645 s for 90 degrees |
| Linear slide, Control Hub port 2 (continuous mode, shot blocker attached) | Axon MAX+ | 0.115 s/60 degrees (521.74 deg/s) | Travel unmeasured; 180-degree placeholder |

Sources: [Axon MAX+ specifications](https://docs.axon-robotics.com/archive/max), [Axon programmer settings](https://docs.axon-robotics.com/archive/programmer), and [goBILDA Super Speed specifications](https://www.gobilda.com/2000-series-dual-mode-servo-25-4-super-speed/). These ratings assume the specified supply voltage; load, acceleration, and internal settings affect actual motion. Two rotation servos provide additional torque, not twice the speed. PWM pulse width selects position, not motor power. The goBILDA gate specifies a 50 Hz (20 ms) PWM frame, which matches the SDK default; increasing that frequency is not a documented speed upgrade.

The Axon programmer settings must be written to each MAX+ separately; this repository cannot read or change them through the FTC `Servo` API. For maximum available output on the robot's 6.0 V servo supply, set **PWM Power to 100%**. Axon permits 100% at 6.0 V, while recommending 85% as its standard setting; at 100%, monitor temperature because thermal protection may shut the servo down. If startup acceleration must also be unrestricted, disable **Soft Start** in the programmer. Keep the existing travel, neutral, and direction settings so home positions are preserved (the depollinator requires increasing PWM = clockwise viewed from the output shaft). Axon servo power comes from the confirmed injector; the Expansion Hub supplies their position commands. Gate power and commands both come directly from the Expansion Hub.

**Turret rotation pair (ports 1 and 2).** Both servos mesh with one turret ring, so they must never turn in different directions or at different speeds, or the gears slip. In software, `PairedServos` gives both servos one direction (`TurretSubsystem.ROTATION_SERVO_DIRECTION`; there is no per-servo direction), one 500–2500 us PWM range and one position, written to both in the same loop. Init fails if the directions or ranges differ. No OpMode can drive one alone: `Calibrate: DISCONNECTED Servo RAW` lists the pair as one entry that moves both. Software cannot set a positional servo's speed; each servo's internal controller chooses it. So both servos need **identical** Axon programmer settings: the same PWM Power, Soft Start, travel/range, neutral/center, direction and dampening. Program both with the same saved profile, and recheck both whenever either servo is replaced or reprogrammed. Mount both in the same orientation on the ring.

Keep Axon's factory **Dampening Factor** and overload protection. The manufacturer advises against changing its internal D coefficient. FTC positional servos expose position commands, not `DcMotorEx` velocity/PIDF setters; adding motor PIDF calls cannot unlock servo speed. Competition turret control uses its external Through-Bore encoder for bounded positional adjustments starting above 3 degrees and stopping below 1.5 degrees, without a repeating external PID. This does not increase the servo speed rating. The other mechanisms have no external measured position feedback, so an added software PID loop would have no measured error to regulate. See the [FTC Servo API](https://javadoc.io/static/org.firstinspires.ftc/RobotCore/10.3.0/com/qualcomm/robotcore/hardware/Servo.html).

Transfer feeding starts 0.10 s after the gate-open command, with no extra settling allowance, and turret homing still waits for encoder stability. These waits do not slow physical movement. Rated travel and hood position estimates do not prove arrival; replace timing allowances with measured loaded values before automatic shooting calibration is enabled.

## Coordinates, samples, and vision

Pinpoint is connected to **Control Hub I2C port 0**, configured as `pinpoint`. The SDK hardware map selects the bus from the active Robot Configuration; no Java bus reassignment is needed.

Shot planning uses **meters, seconds, radians**. The team's field convention is center origin, +X toward blue, +Y toward the scoring table, +yaw counterclockwise. This is a team convention: tag surveys, routes, and starting poses must all use it. Robot axes are +X forward, +Y left. Drivetrain command lateral/yaw are right/clockwise positive and are converted at the navigation boundary. Turret encoder zero is servo home at position 0.5, pointing robot-right. Driver-facing turret headings increase clockwise: front 0, right/home 90, back 180, left 270 degrees; the left 265..275 degree zone is excluded. Internal encoder/solver yaw remains counterclockwise from home (right = -90 degrees in robot math). Camera angles are tx right-positive, ty up-positive. Runtime metric geometry uses each tag's independent `getTargetPoseCameraSpace()` pose: optical +X right, +Y down, +Z forward, rotated through the known camera pitch. Configured high-tag height is a classifier comparison, not an input used to infer range.

`PinpointOdometry` performs one bulk update and publishes a timestamped `MotionSample` with pose, fixed-frame X/Y velocity, angular velocity, linear/angular acceleration estimates, uncertainty, status, and field-reference validity. X/Y velocity uses the same fixed axes as Pinpoint position; it is not rotated by robot heading again. The [manufacturer's output and setPosition documentation](https://www.gobilda.com/content/user_manuals/3110-0002-0001%20User%20Guide.pdf) describes position/velocity outputs and establishing a field reference. Installed displacement/velocity signs and turning behavior remain physical verification dependencies. Constructor zero and diagnostic zero deliberately do not establish a field reference. Competition TeleOps start in local odometry and acquire a field reference from mapped raised tags or GP2 D-pad Left at the alliance corner opposite its pollen depot. Blue uses the audience-right corner facing -X (180 degrees); Red uses the far-left corner facing +X (0 degrees). Rear and side must be against the walls; each wrapper defines `getFieldRehomePose()` using the shared robot dimensions. Corner rehome is edge-triggered in INIT and ACTIVE, sets position and heading without recalibrating the IMU, clears prior vision history, and requests a three-second Limelight refinement. The turret points at the nearest alliance hive; five fresh consistent frames refine translation while retaining wall-aligned heading. The fused aiming field pose combines the corner prior with range/area-weighted tag observations and preserves capture-time compensation. Pending refinement inhibits automatic feed; motion or manual turret control cancels it, and timeout retains the corner-only pose. See [refinement checks and telemetry](CompetitionTeleOp.md). Approved autonomous strategies retain their own starting pose. Let INIT calibration finish before START.

`PoseHistory` stores one second of pose and turret yaw, interpolating across heading wrap. Vision corrections modify a translation transform rather than resetting hardware pose or differentiating position jumps. Competition TeleOp uses this corrected estimate for aiming; the match-autonomous adapter disables camera fusion and uses odometry for both navigation and shooting. Current pose/velocity older than 100 ms is rejected. Observations older than 350 ms, missing history, duplicate/out-of-order frames, mismatched target maps, and position jumps over 0.5 m are rejected. Competition TeleOp uses known field openings without a camera-freshness or observed-state firing gate. Periodic corrections are enabled using the nominal manual-derived map and documented mount, limited to one accepted correction per 0.5 s. Individual frames must pass the existing quality, height, freshness and jump checks. Legacy diagnostic adapters retain their 750-ms target-observation and 150-ms state-observation gates. The match-autonomous adapter uses odometry with its route-selected target and no mid-route vision correction. These are configurable software limits, not measured accuracy guarantees.

SDK 12 bytecode was inspected locally: `LLResult.getControlHubTimeStamp()` uses `System.currentTimeMillis()` at parsing start; `getControlHubTimeStampNanos()` merely scales that wall-clock value. The result's Limelight-local timestamp identifies duplicate frames. `PoseHistory.captureTime` bridges wall-clock receipt age to `System.nanoTime()`, subtracting capture and pipeline latency once. JSON parse time is already included in receipt age. See the [SDK latency/timestamp API](https://javadoc.io/static/org.firstinspires.ftc/Hardware/10.1.0/com/qualcomm/hardware/limelightvision/LLResult.html) for the meanings of these latency fields. Enter any measured additional transport delay in `ADDITIONAL_VISION_TRANSPORT_SECONDS`; do not add staleness or parse latency again. `VISION_TIMING_MEASURED` remains false for legacy diagnostic firing. Competition TeleOp relocalization is enabled using SDK capture/pipeline timing and the existing zero additional-transport estimate; measured transport delay can refine it.

The former MegaTag camera/robot-pose conflation and competing direct-centering loops were removed. Field relocalization uses metric per-tag observations and the selected target's nominal drawing-derived raised-tag map plus the rotating camera mount. With a verified local tag-to-opening transform, target-relative aiming uses the local odometry frame without requiring an absolute map. It never assumes a Limelight `botpose` is a camera position.

Automatic readiness requires valid homing/target/pose/profile/timing, yaw error <=3 degrees, estimated hood tracking, and flywheel speed over the existing 150-ms readiness window. Chassis velocity, chassis acceleration and turret rate do not gate feeding. Drivers choose whether to fire while moving. Entry/retention tolerances and timed recovery avoid gate chatter. Invalid solutions stop further feeding immediately. A ball already committed may still exit; telemetry reports that possibility and **timed feed attempts**, never invented ball counts. ### Turret manual hold and external encoder backup

Competition GP2 RS-X requests linear yaw velocity up to the rated 6 V speed of 521.74 deg/s through 1:1 gearing. Two 10-ms velocity filters briefly ease acceleration changes; the shared 5-ms position-command smoothing softens target changes. These command filters do not guarantee physical acceleration or jerk. The positional servos use their own internal feedback. On stick or auto-aim release, competition control captures measured yaw once and discards queued target travel; subsequent encoder noise cannot move that hold target. Diagnostic tests retain their previous commanded-target hold.

Competition correction starts only above 3 degrees, after same-direction error qualifies for 250 ms at measured turret speed <=3 deg/s. While active it makes bounded position adjustments with another settled interval between them. It stops adjusting below 1.5 degrees and retains the successful offset; it does not restart until error exceeds 3 degrees. Total offset remains bounded to 25 degrees and mechanical travel. Target changes above 3 degrees reset the correction episode; small odometry target noise does not. These correction qualifications do not gate feeding. Automatic feed accepts <=3-degree yaw error without any turret-rate gate.

Loop order: `update()` reads the turret encoder, flywheel and Limelight and advances the hood estimate; `applyYawCommand()` writes the yaw servos after this loop's auto-aim, manual or hold target is set. All turret OpModes and the autonomous output coordinator call it once per loop. The auto-aim servo command leads the pose by `TurretControl.AIM_LEAD_SECONDS` (actuation delay, starting estimate 25 ms, unmeasured); readiness keeps the current bearing. Competition TeleOp does not cap chassis turning while GP1 RT auto-aims. See [measurement steps](CompetitionTeleOp.md).

GP1 RT prepares tracking of the nearest alliance cell using odometry even without a shot profile; RT+LT requests automatic firing. Hood/RPM interpolation and automatic firing use the measured shot profile; release timing does not gate firing. There is no camera-centering fallback in competition TeleOp. The turret-only test and other diagnostic adapters remain separate.

Competition TeleOp INIT applies a same-alliance autonomous end pose from `AutoPoseHandoff` (at most `MAX_AGE_SECONDS` = 60 s old, after the Pinpoint has been READY for `PINPOINT_SETTLE_SECONDS` = 0.5 s, which is `PinpointOdometry.READY_SETTLE_SECONDS`), so odometry aiming is field-referenced from the first loop; GP2 D-pad Left replaces it and follows the same READY-settle rule (`CornerRehomeRequest`). Test: Auto Aim does not use the handoff. See [competition TeleOp](CompetitionTeleOp.md).

### Turret and Limelight mounting

The team's corrected mounting description has the Limelight facing the same direction as the muzzle (relative yaw 0 degrees), with its lens axis pitched 55 degrees above horizontal (front glass team-measured 55 degrees from vertical on 2026-11-05; previously documented as 30), with its lens 337 mm above the floor (team-measured 2026-11-06; was 335). The turret pivot is centered left/right and 50.68184865 mm forward of robot center. With the muzzle pointing robot-forward, the camera is centered left/right and 99.53073974 mm behind the turret pivot. Its displacement rotates with the turret. The camera is therefore 48.84889109 mm behind robot center only when the muzzle points robot-forward. These confirmed values are recorded in ShootingConfiguration.MOUNT with measured=true. Target geometry, shot profiles and timing retain their separate calibration requirements.

The ball exit is centered on the turret rotation axis in plan view. Hood movement does not move the camera or significantly move the exit position; it changes the exit angle.
